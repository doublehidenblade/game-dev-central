## Craig's direction and the recommendation boundary

**Confirmed direction from the current request:** recognize Cloudpunk as a direct reference; distinguish Kurogane Bay through its Showa retro-futurism and more systemic replayability; procedural work generated by supply and demand; raw goods reaching downstream businesses, passengers and contraband; time/police/bandit/rival pressure; meaningful large loads versus fast repeated runs; bring money and resources home for vehicle/garage growth, eventually trucks/fleet and rented parking. Intercity arbitrage comes later; enormous price gaps within this small city are not the premise. Reduce the burden of authoring and reviewing branching stories while retaining authored worldbuilding.

**Recommendations for review, not new user mandates:** a bounded deterministic economy, two production chains first, three at most before expansion, one new pressure layer at a time, forgiving ordinary jobs beside premium timed work, discrete cargo slots plus mass, a small recipe set, and abstract fleet dispatch before physical followers. All quantities, bands, timings and acceptance targets below are provisional tuning hypotheses. No claim that adding simulation guarantees replayability or commercial differentiation.

**Preserved canon:** the six Lower City districts, original masked cast, warm ochre dust and analog Showa machines, Upper City above the clouds, Kuro-Kiri restrictions on unsuited outdoor travel with suited workers at their posts permitted. The wheelman remains in the cab. Navigation remains a pre-job briefing, signs, landmarks and paper map; ROUTE-88 is a manifest/settlement terminal, not live GPS. Existing art is retained; no new generated art or provider calls.

The comparison is about documented emphasis, not a claim that competitors have no other systems. Sources accessed/researched 2026-10-06; dated statements retain their dates. Craig's “cozy” reading is a tonal preference, not a verified universal description of Cloudpunk.

|---|---|---|
| Cloudpunk base game | Official store describes Rania's first night, story-led exploration, characters and decisions, with a hovercar and on-foot vertical city exploration. [S1] | Delivery work is already a direct sci-fi reference. Borrow atmosphere and a readable working city; test repeatable load/route/business consequences as our emphasis. Ground vehicles, Showa signage and staying at the wheel reinforce identity. |
| Cloudpunk upkeep | Official achievements explicitly cover refueling, repairs, vehicle upgrades and time-sensitive deliveries. [S2] | Fuel, vehicle damage, repair, upgrades and some timed deliveries are shared mechanics, not differentiation claims. Police/checkpoint fiction does not establish the presence or absence of a systemic wanted/pursuit model; leave that unverified. |
| City of Ghosts DLC | The separate 2021 campaign advertises street races and HOVA customization. [S4] | Do not claim Cloudpunk lacks races or customization. Do not conflate the separate Nivalis business-sim project with this base game or DLC. |

**Positioning hypothesis:** “Read what the city needs, assemble a worthwhile load, choose a route and its risks, then bring home the means to improve your operation.” A quieter ordinary shift and a tense voluntary premium job can coexist. Prove the choices are interesting with dialogue disabled before increasing story volume.

## Roadmap: reuse the current city

This is a scope recommendation, not authorization to implement or cancel another worker's work. `td-185` already supplies the city/sign/map/gig foundation; traffic and cops have separate current tasks. Preserve implemented passenger/cargo behavior and police integration. The phases below describe additional economy work and its gates, not a replacement city or a reset of shipped progress.

| Phase | Bounded addition | Gate before expanding |
|---|---|---|
| 0 — preserve the foundation | Inventory current gig, save, damage, traffic and arrest behavior on the accepted game head. Exercise the existing map and signs. | Document actual interfaces and remaining defects with owners; don't import design promises as implemented facts. |
| 1 — local economy proof | In the existing city use 6 businesses, 4 goods, 2 recipes, one starter vehicle plus two load/module configurations, contract freight and existing passenger jobs. Fixed ticks, short manifests, receipts, garage storage/one upgrade, two-stop batching. No whole-population or off-map simulation. | Stock/settlement invariants and ordinary-job recovery pass; players can explain a downstream consequence and choose between heavy and light runs. |
| 2 — varied shifts | Optional third chain, owned trading stock, a second vehicle, comfort/perishable/contraband modifiers and one legible pressure layer at a time. Reuse existing police work subject to its acceptance; rivals first compete for unreserved work, bandits first use warned road events. | No repeatable money/resource loop; both vehicle niches survive; risk is understood before commitment. |
| 3 — distant depot trade | A constrained Nagisa/terminal market with a shipping schedule, real travel cost and finite imports; only then a separate intercity market, after map and handling gates. Rented parking and bounded abstract driver dispatch. | Longer routes earn margins through separation, time and access; wages/upkeep/parking remain legible and player driving remains worthwhile. |
| 4 — optional expansion | Physical following trucks, convoy recovery/formation AI, additional cities, faction territory simulation, full vehicle combat. | Separate feasibility and fun reviews for pathfinding, blocked junctions, save consistency, performance and combat. None is needed to prove Phase 1. |

A solo developer using AI still owns balance, persistence, asset rights, route QA and readable feedback. Deterministic tables and authored constraints reduce debugging/review surface; unlimited procedural text, many-agent economies and combat multiply it. No runtime generative dialogue or paid generation is required by this design.

## Proposed playtest gates

These are **future tests**, not results of this documentation PR. Start with five first-time players, two 25-minute shifts each, at least three fixed economy seeds; log offers, decisions, cargo, route time, settlements and balances. Small-sample gates are formative, not statistical proof.

| Question | Concrete pass target / failure response |
|---|---|
| Can players read the economy? | At least 4/5 explain one input → output → customer relationship and identify a visible stock change after delivery without an explanation from the tester. If not, simplify the chain/UI. |
| Can they navigate? | At least 4/5 complete two ordinary jobs using briefing, signs and paper map without tester help or GPS; record wrong turns and illegible signs against the existing road plan. |
| Are load choices meaningful? | Across heavy consolidated and light urgent scenarios, each strategy wins net earnings per minute in at least one authored benchmark; at least 4/5 explain the tradeoff. Neither vehicle/configuration dominates every tested demand state. |
| Is work fair and recoverable? | At least 80% of ordinary contracts completed by shift two; at least 4/5 identify deadline, condition risk and likely net costs before acceptance. From zero cash plus a disabled vehicle, every test seed permits recovery and a paid basic job within 10 minutes, with positive net afterward. |
| Does progression feel attainable? | First useful upgrade after 3–6 ordinary successful jobs in reference tuning; after fitting it, players identify its benefit and cost/limitation. Keep baseline work viable without the upgrade. |
| Does replay survive less story? | At least 4/5 encounter a different useful manifest or route on shift two, and 3/5 voluntarily select another job during an optional five-minute extension with dialogue disabled. Repetition or confusion triggers redesign before more content. |
| Is accounting safe? | A proposed 1,000-shift seeded soak has no negative stock, duplicate job settlement, unbounded queues or unrecoverable economy. Save/reload and duplicate-event tests preserve ledger hashes at the same tick. Test cancel/return/resell/repair/refund/garage crafting and NPC/player simultaneous claims explicitly. |
| Does time remain predictable? | Same seed + action/tick log produces identical ledger; pause/save/scene changes do not advance simulation secretly. Test a tick boundary during pickup and delivery. Measure budget on target hardware before adding businesses; this PR makes no performance claim. |

## Remaining decisions for Craig

1. **Pressure:** how relaxed should ordinary work be, and which premium-job risk leads after existing police fixes settle: inspections, rival bookings, or warned bandit roadblocks? Recommendation: keep a safe legal baseline and tune inspections first.
2. **Ownership:** permit player-owned commodity trading in Phase 2, or keep all MVP cargo as service contracts? Recommendation: contracts first so a driver cannot lose all working capital on the first run.
3. **Garage rewards:** cash plus optional legal salvage, or explicit materials on selected contracts? Recommendation: two material inputs with shop substitutes; no mandatory random-part grind.
4. **Clock and failure:** accept a paused planning screen, 10-second economy ticks and bounded fees/recovery service? These are proposed rules, not retroactive changes to the active arrest task.
5. **Fleet end state:** is managing hired drivers sufficient initially, or is seeing following trucks essential to the fantasy? Recommendation: rent bays/abstract dispatch first; do not promise physical convoys in the slice.
6. **Legacy ambitions:** keep the war-map endgame, six chassis/five art tiers and specialist cargo as future art direction, pending separate gates? This update recommends staging rather than deleting them.
