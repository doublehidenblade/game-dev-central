### 1. The "Night-Flip" Fallacy (Why Daytime Breaks When Darkened)

* **Linework Obliteration:** 80s daytime cel depends on 100% black ink lines contrasting against high-luminance surfaces (75–95% value). Darkening shifts surfaces into the 10–30% value range, swallowing ink lines and destroying silhouette clarity.
* **The "Neon Soup" Trap:** When a daylight composition loses its value read, artists panic and drop in cyan/magenta point lights and emissive signage. This creates a generic cyberpunk smear that kills graphic cel readability.
* **Atmospheric Scattering Reversal:** Daylight air perspective uses desaturated, high-key warm scattering (sky-wash). Darkening this turns atmospheric depth into a toxic, muddy purple fog.
* **Rule:** If a scene is designed for noon, its exposure, local values, and cel separations exist *only* for noon. Never slap a night color grade on a daytime layout.

### 2. Sun-Bleached Concrete: Warm Midday Cel-Shading

Massive concrete structures under a midday sun must never register as cool grey or lavender. Treat them like classic hand-painted poster-color backgrounds (*Nicker* gouache on board).

```
[Sunlight Face]      Pale Ivory / Sandstone / Bone (Warm 5–10% yellow bias)
      │
[Step 1 Shadow]      Dry Ochre-Grey / Muted Khaki (Warm terminator, value 65%)
      │
[Step 2 Shadow]      Muted Cerulean Slate (Sky ambient bounce, value 45%, NEVER purple)
```

* **Preventing Sterility Without Textures:**
  * **Formwork & P-Con Holes:** Break blank mega-surfaces using structural geometry: 1m × 2m panel seams, recessed chamfers, and tie-rod holes rendered with crisp ink marks.
  * **Vertical Weathering Vectors:** High altitudes bake surfaces chalky-white; moisture streaks are tight, vertical soot trails clustered *strictly* beneath drainage spouts, expansion joints, and parapet caps.
  * **Sky Reflection on Glazing:** Massive vertical ribbon windows do not use realistic cubemaps. Use solid, high-key cobalt-to-cyan gradient bands broken by razor-sharp white diagonal glare strikes.

### 3. "Nothing Deleted by the Look": Preserving Scale

Stylization in 80s anime means **simplified rendering**, not **omitted engineering**. Removing small-scale functional hardware destroys the megastructure's sense of gigantism.

* **Non-Negotiable Scale Anchors:**
  * **Viaduct Anatomy:** Retain jagged finger-plate expansion joints, elastomeric bearing pads between pier and deck, and exterior utility conduits running along the bridge deck soffit.
  * **Maintenance Infrastructure:** Hang external steel cat-walks, safety-caged vertical ladders, and inspection gantries beneath road decks.
  * **The "Human Metric":** Emergency call boxes (*Hijō Denwa*), maintenance door hatches, and 85cm-tall crash barriers are subconscious measuring sticks. Without them, a 100-meter-tall viaduct reads as an injection-molded plastic toy.

### 4. Showa-Era (1955–1989) Expressway Anchors

Ground the elevated highway in late-Showa retro-futurism rather than generic, sleek sci-fi:

* **Overhead Gantries:** Heavy structural steel angle-iron lattice trusses, painted industrial dark moss green or zinc-dipped silver. Gantries are cluttered with square maintenance walkways and back-mounted transformer boxes.
* **Signage:** Deep green enamel signboards featuring white Japanese Highway Gothic (*JH Gothic*) characters and English Helvetica; framed by high-contrast white retro-reflective outer borders. Include yellow triangular warning signs with pictograms.
* **Sound Barriers & Guardrails:**
  * Lower section: Standard corrugated W-beam steel barriers on square H-beam posts.
  * Upper section: Pale green frames holding yellowed, weathered acrylic acoustic panels with exposed rubber gaskets.
* **Lighting Infrastructure:** Tapered steel masts carrying curved "cobra-head" fixtures housing high-pressure sodium lamps. Daylight appearance: dirty unlit glass lenses showing faint orange-tinted reflectors inside.
* **Pier Retrofits:** Massive circular or fluted-rectangular concrete columns wrapped at their bases with thick riveted steel seismic jacketing painted oxidized red-orange primer or utility grey.
