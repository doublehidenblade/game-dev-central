# KUROGANE BAY (黒鉄湾) — CANON WORLD BIBLE

---

## (A) THE CITY: KUROGANE BAY

**Identity:**  
Kurogane Bay is an unzoned, reclaimed industrial megaport built on the ashes of a collapsed wartime empire, where neon-drenched Showa-era canal alleys collide with monolithic, vacuum-tube skyscraper sprawls. It is a city that never pauses to sleep because its blast furnaces, pneumatic mail networks, and deep-water container docks run on mandatory round-the-clock shift cycles. To the outside world, Kurogane is an economic miracle fueled by synthetic fuel refining and micro-relay electronics; to its citizens, it is an iron pressure-cooker where street syndicates, shipping barons, and hungry gig drivers fight for every square meter of asphalt.

**Economic Engine & The Driver's Niche:**  
Kurogane Bay exists because of **Sol-88** (a high-density synthetic kerosene refined from deep-sea coal seams) and its status as the hemisphere's only tariff-free deepwater harbor. Raw ores, black-market cathode semiconductors, and livestock pour into the docks; refined synthetic fuels, heavy machinery, and consumer electronics pour out. 

However, the city's geographical layout—a nightmare of sunken canal roads, elevated tollways, and labyrinthine reclaimed alleys built without central urban planning—makes large-scale automated transit impossible. The city lives and dies by **Point-to-Point Wheelmen**: independent drivers who carry high-priority cargo, illicit envelopes, fragile black-market goods, and desperate passengers through dense traffic and turf checkpoints faster than the gridlocked municipal tramways can move.

## (B) THE DISTRICTS

```
              [NORTH: HAIBARA CLIFFS]
                        |
       [DAIKOKU PIERS]--+--[KAMOME WHOLESALE]
              |         |         |
              +---[TENJIN CORRIDOR]---+
              |         |         |
      [KOTOBUKI DOCKS]--+--[CHIDORI NEON STRIP]
                        |
             [SOUTH: SHINKAI BASIN]
```

### 1. Daikoku Piers (大黒埠頭)
* **Identity:** Miles of rusted gantry cranes, stacked shipping containers, and open-flame shipbreaking yards.
* **Business:** Heavy freight logistics, ship salvage, bulk ore smuggling, and industrial dry-dock repairs.
* **Controller:** The **Kaiun-gumi** (Daikoku Longshore Syndicate).
* **Gig Profile:** Heavy-load cargo hauls, smuggling untaxed crate shipments past corrupt port inspectors, and fast emergency medical runs for injured dockers.

### 2. Kamome Wholesale Ward (かもめ市場)
* **Identity:** A chaotic, wet-pavement commercial labyrinth of multi-level seafood halls, cold-storage warehouses, and street-auction stalls.
* **Business:** Food distribution, synthetic ice sales, daily produce auctions, and informal payday loans.
* **Controller:** **Tsuru-kai** (Wholesale Merchants' Mutual Aid).
* **Gig Profile:** Time-sensitive perishables (tuna crates, live octopus tanks that spill if you drift too hard), restaurant resupply runs, and escorting market accountants carrying cash bags before sunrise.

### 3. Chidori Neon Strip (千鳥街)
* **Identity:** Multi-tiered red-light alleyways choked with cabaret clubs, capsule motels, pachinko palaces, and glowing plastic storefronts.
* **Business:** Nightlife, hostess clubs, illegal gambling dens, synthetic liquor parlors, and back-alley plastic surgery clinics.
* **Controller:** **Gokuraku-kai** (The Pleasure Quarter Union).
* **Gig Profile:** VIP VIP cabaret transfers, chasing down skipping debtors, escorting off-duty hostesses through rival territory, and quiet pickups of drunk corporate execs who can never be seen in this district.

### 4. Tenjin Mid-Corridor (天神中央通)
* **Identity:** A brutalist concrete canyon of trading houses, mid-tier banks, insurance firms, and buzzing pneumatic dispatch offices.
* **Business:** Corporate bureaucracy, customs clearing, paper commodities trading, and white-collar vice.
* **Controller:** Contested ground; strictly policed by municipal patrolmen paid off by corporate security firms.
* **Gig Profile:** Time-critical document deliveries between warring trading firms, chauffeuring mid-level executives to secret offshore settlement meets, and running counter-surveillance routes to shake tailing corporate spies.

### 5. Kotobuki Slums & Repair Row (寿町)
* **Identity:** A sunken shantytown built beneath elevated expressway supports, dense with chop shops, unlicensed radio stations, and surplus markets.
* **Business:** Black-market vehicle tuning, unregistered electronics assembly, counterfeit papers, and gray-market fuel distribution.
* **Controller:** **The Iron Wheel Union (Tetsurin-rengo)**.
* **Gig Profile:** Sourcing obscure car parts across town on a timer, hot-drop parcel deliveries with zero questions asked, and getaway runs for mechanics running from cartel collection squads.

### 6. Haibara Heights (灰原高台)
* **Identity:** A secluded, cliffside zone of high-walled modernist estates, corporate executive villas, and private gardens overlooking the bay's smog.
* **Business:** Private equity, political lobbying, cartel money laundering, and high-society auctions.
* **Controller:** Unofficial demilitarized zone owned by the **Tycoons**; enforced by private armed security.
* **Gig Profile:** High-fare luxury limousine gigs, discreet delivery of delicate personal items (blackmail tapes, rare vintage wine, pedigree pets), and dead-silent passenger rides where the driver must never look in the rearview mirror.

### 7. Shinkai Basin (深海低地)
* **Identity:** A foul-smelling swamp of Sol-88 refining vats, cracking plants, and chemical drainage canals illuminated by flare stacks.
* **Business:** Synthetic fuel refining, bulk plastics, toxic waste handling, and industrial sabotage.
* **Controller:** Run directly by **Kurogane Petro-Chemical (KPC)** security contractors with underground Kaiun-gumi ties.
* **Gig Profile:** Volatile fuel-canister deliveries (hard acceleration or impacts raise explosion risk), transporting factory whistleblowers out of the zone, and midnight toxic-sludge disposal runs.

## (C) THE FACTIONS

### 1. The Kaiun-gumi (海運組 — The Ocean Transport Syndicate)
* **Origin:** Formed by demobilized naval logistics personnel and deepwater dockers during the Reconstruction to clear harbor debris; evolved into the brute-force masters of the waterfront.
* **Territory:** Daikoku Piers, Outer Anchorages, Freight Gates 1 through 9.
* **Rackets:** Port stevedore extortion, container theft, maritime smuggling (weapons, untaxed whiskey, bulk steel), and heavy equipment chop shops.
* **Active Rivalry:** Blood feud with the **Tsuru-kai** over control of Kamome Harbor's cold-storage customs gates.
* **Leader:** **Oyabun Ryuzo "The Winch" Murata** — A gravel-voiced, fifty-year-old bear of a man missing his left ear from a cable snap. He views Kurogane Bay as *his* private pond and treats every cargo container that moves without his chalk-mark as an act of war. He is strictly old-school: respects drivers who do not flinch, but will sink a car in the bay over an unpaid harbor toll.

### 2. The Gokuraku-kai (極楽会 — The Paradise Society)
* **Origin:** Emerged from the post-war black-market dance halls and street cabaret rings; built their fortune on hospitality, synthetic vice, and show business.
* **Territory:** The Chidori Neon Strip, North Canal Motel Row.
* **Rackets:** Hostess clubs, illegal card parlors, synthetic stimulant distribution ("Spark-9"), celebrity blackmail, and municipal licensing kickbacks.
* **Active Rivalry:** Cold proxy war with the **Iron Wheel Union** over the transport and supply networks that enter Chidori.
* **Leader:** **Madame Hanayo Kanzaki** — Elegant, sharp-tongued, constantly smoking thin charcoal-filtered cigarettes inside her velvet-lined office above the Starlight Lounge. Hanayo values style, decorum, and absolute discretion. She uses cab drivers as her personal intelligence network, paying handsomely for eavesdropped passenger gossip.

### 3. The Tsuru-kai (鶴会 — The Market Crane Brotherhood)
* **Origin:** Began as an armed mutual-aid collective of wholesale fishmongers and grain peddlers protecting their supply wagons from looters during the Great Fuel Famine.
* **Territory:** Kamome Wholesale Ward, North Rail Depots.
* **Rackets:** Daily produce price-fixing, synthetic ice monopolies, short-term extortionate loans to small merchants, and freight lane toll stations.
* **Active Rivalry:** At war with the **Kaiun-gumi** over dockside cold-chain facilities; actively choking supplies to Kaiun-affiliated mess halls.
* **Leader:** **"Abacus" Kenjiro Sato** — A dry, deceptively mild accountant who dresses in traditional merchant aprons over expensive bespoke wool suits. Sato does not carry a firearm; he carries a brass abacus and an ledgers book. He destroys rivals through debt, supply starvation, and razor-wire ambushes along critical supply avenues.

### 4. The Iron Wheel Union (鉄輪連合 — Tetsurin-rengo)
* **Origin:** Born from rogue courier drivers, illegal midnight drag-racers, and wildcat chop-shop mechanics who refused to bend to the harbor syndicates.
* **Territory:** Kotobuki Slums, the Under-Expressway Ring, Southern Canal Shallows.
* **Rackets:** Black-market vehicle modifications, underground pink-slip street racing, unregistered courier dispatches, and stolen fuel siphoning.
* **Active Rivalry:** Constant territorial skirmishes with the municipal traffic police and extortion squads sent by the Gokuraku-kai.
* **Leader:** **Boss Tetsu (Tetsuo "Camshaft" Ogata)** — A grease-covered mechanical savant whose spine is held together by surgical steel rods. He speaks in short, mechanic-jargon bursts. Tetsu treats the roads of Kurogane as a sacred commons; anyone who drives well earns his respect, but corporate or syndicate thugs attempting to regulate his garages find their brake lines cut.

## (D) THE ECONOMY

```
[RAW IMPORTS / ORE / CRUDE] 
         │ (Daikoku Piers - Kaiun-gumi)
         ▼
[KPC REFINERIES: Sol-88 Synthetic Fuel] 
         │
         ├───► [KOTOBUKI CHOP SHOPS: Street Modding & Gray Fuel]
         │
         ├───► [KAMOME MARKET: Cold-Chain Logistics & Food Supply]
         │              │
         │              ▼
         └───► [CHIDORI ENTERTAINMENT: Vice & Cash Laundering]
                        │
                        ▼
         [TENJIN BANKS & TYCOONS: Clean Capital Outflow]
```

### The 5 Systemic Money Flows
1. **The Harbor Siphon:** Ships dock at Daikoku Piers -> Kaiun-gumi levies "off-manifest protection fees" -> Unregistered cargo moves via midnight vans to Kotobuki chop shops -> Refurbished luxury items/parts are sold in Chidori and Tenjin at 300% markup.
2. **The Synthetic Bloodline:** Deep-sea coal/crude lands at Shinkai Basin -> Kurogane Petro-Chemical refines Sol-88 fuel -> Municipal tankers distribute to licensed stations; syndicate fuel thieves siphon off 15% to supply the underground delivery network and street racing scene.
3. **The Morning Wholesale Squeeze:** Tsuru-kai controls all food entering Kamome Market -> Demands cash kickbacks from delivery haulers -> Fresh cuts go to Haibara Heights estates; canned/processed scraps go to Kotobuki; cash flow laundered through Tenjin paper companies.
4. **The Neon Wash:** Chidori clubs generate massive, untraceable daily physical cash from gambling and hostesses -> Money is carried across town via cab drivers to avoid electronic scrutiny -> Deposited into Tenjin commercial banks under shell transport firms.
5. **The Paper Chase:** Corporate tycoons in Tenjin generate speculative stock fortunes -> Pay off-the-books courier drivers to move physical bond certificates and blackmail material ahead of municipal regulatory raids.

### The Tycoons Above the Streets
* **Baroness Chiyo Moriyama (Head of Moriyama Heavy Logistics):** Controls the shipping lines, gantry manufacturing, and the municipal harbor lease. The Kaiun-gumi think they run the docks; Moriyama owns the literal land the docks sit on and sets the shipping tariffs that dictate whether the syndicates feast or starve.
* **Director Hidetaka Vance (CEO of Kurogane Petro-Chemical):** A half-foreign industrialist who holds a government-backed monopoly on synthetic Sol-88 refining. Cold, technocratic, and indifferent to human cost, Vance treats the entire city as an engine that consumes fuel and lives to produce export value.
* **Councilman Goro Minami (President of the Metropolitan Transport Board):** The political architect of Kurogane's unzoned street grid. Minami deliberately suppresses public rail expansion so his family-owned commercial bus networks, private tollway concessions, and taxi monopolies can extract tolls from every wheel moving in the city.

## (E) RECURRING CAST

### 1. "Auntie" Shizue Miyamoto (The Morning Dispatcher)
* **Role:** Chief dispatcher for the "Asahi Radio Cab & Delivery Co."
* **Personality:** World-weary, chain-chewing throat-lozenge addict who treats drivers like wayward sons while barking dispatch orders like an artillery commander.
* **Gig Situations:** The player's primary early-game gig provider; throws steady cab pickups and grocery hauls your way, occasionally slipping in "a personal favor for a cousin" that spirals into a syndicate mess.

### 2. Taro "Clean Towel" Inaba (The Hostess Club Driver)
* **Role:** Senior private driver for the Gokuraku-kai's premier cabaret lounges.
* **Personality:** Pathologically discreet, immaculately groomed, speaks entirely in polite honorifics even while scrubbing blood off leather upholstery.
* **Gig Situations:** Hands off high-paying night-shift VIP routes when he is double-booked; calls the player for high-speed intercepts when an unhinged patron stalks a high-earning hostess.

### 3. Inspector Kenichi Dan (The Exhaust Detective)
* **Role:** Demoted, rumpled municipal traffic detective who practically lives in his unmarked cruiser.
* **Personality:** Cynical, exhausted, driven by a stubborn core of real justice; smells of stale black canned coffee and tobacco.
* **Gig Situations:** Flags the player down as a regular cab fare to silently tail suspicious vehicles, run plate numbers across districts, or beat his own corrupt superiors to crime scenes.

### 4. Mariko "Zero-Gauge" Katsu (The Underground Tuner)
* **Role:** Kotobuki's most sought-after mechanic; runs a garage hidden inside an abandoned train shed.
* **Personality:** Hyperactive, obsessed with carburetion and twin-scroll turbos, completely indifferent to legal codes or personal danger.
* **Gig Situations:** Sends the player on high-stress speed trials to test experimental engine mods across tollways; needs ultra-rare parts fetched from rival territory under tight timers.

### 5. Kenji the Fish-King (The Kamome Auctioneer)
* **Role:** The undisputed king of the dawn bluefin tuna auction.
* **Personality:** Belligerent, booming voice, wears rubber boots year-round and screams every sentence as if competing with boat horns.
* **Gig Situations:** Fragile, high-speed cargo gigs: delivering million-yen fresh tuna carcasses to elite Haibara restaurants before rigor mortis sets in; hard braking ruins the meat quality and docks your pay.

### 6. Sister Beatrice (The Slum Clinic Operator)
* **Role:** Foreign medical missionary running an unlicensed emergency surgical ward in the Kotobuki shanties.
* **Personality:** Deadpan, unsqueamish, completely unfazed by syndicate warlords or bullet wounds.
* **Gig Situations:** Emergency medical runs: picking up critically wounded drivers or dockers from back-alleys without notifying the municipal police; rush deliveries of black-market plasma bags.

### 7. Shinji "Slippery" Uno (The Tenjin White-Collar Mule)
* **Role:** Professional bagman for Tenjin's mid-level corporate suits.
* **Personality:** Sweaty, neurotically punctual, checking three different gold watches on his wrist.
* **Gig Situations:** High-stakes document and briefcases handoffs: time-critical deliveries between corporate HQs where rival corporate muscle cars attempt to ram you off the road to force contract defaults.

### 8. The "Record-Shop Phantom" (Audiophile Courier)
* **Role:** Eccentric collector of rare, fragile 1970s analog vinyl and master magnetic tapes.
* **Personality:** Whispering recluse who views modern digital audio as an insult to God; demands total physical care over speed.
* **Gig Situations:** Ultra-gentle delivery gigs: navigating pothole-ridden alleys without exceeding specific G-force thresholds; any hard impact, curb scratch, or collision destroys the payload's audio integrity.

### 9. Lieutenant Kuga (The Kaiun-gumi Heavy)
* **Role:** Dockside enforcer for Oyabun Murata.
* **Personality:** Cold-eyed, scar across his nose, rarely raises his voice because his iron pipe usually does the talking.
* **Gig Situations:** Muscle delivery: requires the player to transport bruised, silent faction members to secret drop points; offers high payouts for ramming rival scout cars blocking dock choke points.

### 10. Little Sparrow (The Street Informant)
* **Role:** Thirteen-year-old shoeshiner and pneumatic-chute messenger boy in Chidori.
* **Personality:** Cocky, razor-sharp eyes, talks a mile a minute to conceal deep-seated fear.
* **Gig Situations:** Evasion gigs: jumping into the player's back seat while pursued by syndicate foot soldiers; requires the player to navigate narrow service alleys and pedestrian arcades to shake them.

### 11. Mrs. Yamashita (The Ghost Regular)
* **Role:** An eighty-year-old widow who lives in the hills and calls a cab every Tuesday at precisely 11:15 AM.
* **Personality:** Sweet, painfully polite, hands the driver home-wrapped barley tea candies; oblivious to the city's madness.
* **Gig Situations:** Wholesome yet tense slow-driving gigs: requires peaceful, smooth navigation through chaotic gang-war zones to visit her late husband's grave, providing a grounding moral heart to the world.

## (F) SCRIPTED MISSION PREMISES

### 1. "The Scent of Osmanthus"
* **The Client:** Shingo Hayashi, a mid-level manager at an import insurance firm, clutching a bouquet of expensive flowers and a boutique gift bag.
* **The Complication:** Hayashi is twenty minutes late for his anniversary dinner with his mistress at a luxury Haibara restaurant, but he just realized he left his wife’s blood-pressure medication in the glove compartment of his family sedan back in Kamome. His wife has a severe heart condition; the mistress will leave him if he misses the dinner.
* **The Turn:** As you race to resolve his logistics, Hayashi receives a call: his wife collapsed and is being rushed to the very hospital located directly opposite the restaurant. The player must choose whether to floor it to the hospital to deliver the meds, or drop him at the restaurant to save his double life, while Hayashi suffers an absolute, agonizing moral breakdown in the back seat.

### 2. "Paternal Radar"
* **The Client:** Oyabun Ryuzo Murata himself, disguised poorly in a beige trench coat, dark sunglasses, and a fisherman's bucket hat.
* **The Complication:** His eighteen-year-old daughter—whom he told he was a simple commercial shipping manager—is on her first romantic date with a young, broke mechanic. Murata hires the player's cab to tail their scooter through town without blowing his cover.
* **The Turn:** While following them through Chidori, a hit-squad from the Tsuru-kai spots Murata’s recognizable frame in your back seat. The player must evade the ambush and run the attackers off the road while driving smoothly enough that Murata's daughter never realizes her father is fifty meters behind her gunning down rival syndicates.

### 3. "The Prime Catch"
* **The Client:** Kenji the Fish-King, holding the winning ticket for a 220-kilo wild bluefin tuna at 4:30 AM.
* **The Complication:** The Kaiun-gumi has blockaded the main exit gates of Kamome Wholesale due to an ongoing port fee dispute; the fish must reach the head chef at the Grand Bay Hotel in Tenjin before the 5:30 AM morning breakfast press conference.
* **The Turn:** Kenji loads the massive tuna into the back of your cab, strapping it down with cargo ropes. The ice is melting rapidly, reducing your traction and making the rear slide out on tight turns. To beat the clock, the player must bypass the gang-controlled toll roads by driving along the drainage maintenance canals and launching across an unfinished ramp bridge.

### 4. "Discreet Extraction"
* **The Client:** Yuri, the undisputed No. 1 hostess at Chidori’s most elite club, the "Palace of Gold."
* **The Complication:** A high-ranking Municipal Police Superintendent has locked himself in her dressing room, drunk, weeping, and threatening to shoot his service weapon into the ceiling because Yuri refused to marry him. Yuri needs an emergency ride out of the district before the police department sends an armed tactical unit to cover up the scandal.
* **The Turn:** When Yuri gets in the car, she reveals she took the Superintendent's service revolver and his notebook containing payoffs from the Gokuraku-kai. Halfway through the route, Gokuraku enforcers pull up alongside your car: they don't want Yuri hurt, but they demand you steer into an alley so they can take the book. The player must choose whether to sell out the passenger for syndicate favor or break through their blockade to honor the fare.

### 5. "The Midnight Kidney"
* **The Client:** Sister Beatrice, carrying a temperature-regulated industrial cold-box connected to a battery pack.
* **The Complication:** The battery pack on the organ-carrier has a mechanical fault; its internal thermometer is rising. If it reaches 8°C, the donor organ is ruined. The car's cigarette lighter socket can power the cooler, but only if the vehicle maintains a constant alternator output above 3,500 RPM.
* **The Turn:** The drop point is across the city in Kotobuki, but the route is jammed with early-morning truck traffic and police speed checkpoints. The driver must maintain high revs and drift through tight urban turns without hitting vehicles or stalling, turning the entire city into an emergency slalom where dropping below high speeds literally kills a patient.

### 6. "The Whistleblower's Route"
* **The Client:** A twitching, bespectacled chemical engineer from Kurogane Petro-Chemical, holding a magnetic storage reel.
* **The Complication:** The engineer discovered that KPC has been dumping thousands of gallons of toxic refining runoff into the Shinkai drainage canals, which is leaking into Kamome's seafood beds. KPC private security cruisers are hunting him.
* **The Turn:** The engineer wants to deliver the reel to Inspector Dan at the Central Precinct. However, halfway there, the car radio crackles: an anonymous voice from Moriyama Heavy Logistics offers the player a life-changing cash transfer over the wire to redirect the fare to a private dock warehouse where the engineer can be "dealt with quietly." The player chooses between pure gig mercenary survival and civic justice.

### 7. "The Maestro's Fragile Masterpiece"
* **The Client:** The Record-Shop Phantom, clutching a lacquer-coated acetate disc—the only surviving recording of a legendary post-war jazz ensemble.
* **The Complication:** The acetate is unbaked and raw; any sudden vertical acceleration (hitting a pothole, bumping a curb) or physical shock exceeding 0.4 Gs will shatter or gouge the grooves permanently.
* **The Turn:** Mid-route, an illegal midnight drag race between Tetsurin-rengo tuners breaks out along the Tenjin Expressway, engulfing your cab in high-speed, weaving traffic. The player must complete the trip under a ticking studio-rental clock while maintaining absolute, velvet-smooth driving control amidst the roar of nitrous engines and near-miss crashes.

## (G) WORLD RULES (THE CANON GUARDRAILS)

### 1. The Concrete Ceiling of Technology (Showa-Futurism Rule)
* **What exists:** Vacuum tubes, cathode-ray monitors, micro-cassette recorders, pneumatic message tubes, analog carburetors, mechanical turbos, and analog radio dispatch.
* **What NEVER exists:** Smartphones, the Internet, GPS maps, drone delivery, modern cybernetics, flat-screen OLEDs, or automated self-driving cars.
* **Canon Impact:** Navigation is done via street signs, physical road maps, memorized landmarks, and Auntie's voice over the analog radio. Deliveries must be made hand-to-hand; data travels on physical magnetic tape or paper ledgers.

### 2. The Yakuza-Game Tone Discipline
* **The Rule:** The world, its characters, and its stakes must be treated with deadly, cinematic seriousness. Characters do not make jokes about video game tropes; they do not break the fourth wall. 
* **Canon Impact:** Absurdity comes from the *collision of high stakes and mundane reality* (e.g., an armed mob enforcer crying tears of sincere emotional agony in your back seat because his imported prize-winning persimmon was bruised when you took a turn too hard). If a character is shot, it hurts; if a business fails, lives are ruined.

### 3. The Sanctity of the Driver's Cab
* **The Rule:** The driver's vehicle is sovereign ground. While factions may threaten, chase, or attempt to force the player off the road, no one simply shoots the driver through the window unprovoked at the start of a ride.
* **Canon Impact:** Kurogane runs on an unwritten, practical code: *couriers and cabbies make the city work.* If a syndicate routinely murders gig drivers, the entire city’s food, fuel, and vice distribution halts. A driver who remains professional and neutral is granted begrudging safe passage—until they choose a side on a run.

### 4. The Geography is Real and Fixed
* **The Rule:** No dynamic, procedural city-shifting. Kurogane Bay is an authored, static, physical open-world space.
* **Canon Impact:** Shortcut mastery matters. That ramp through the market alley, the low-clearance tunnel beneath the Tenjin rail tracks, and the tidal access road at Daikoku Piers exist permanently. The player's primary sense of progression is their own mental map of Kurogane's arteries, choke points, and surface conditions.

### 5. Cash and Gas Rule Everything
* **The Rule:** Every transaction in Kurogane is tied directly to physical, paper yen and liters of Sol-88 synthetic kerosene.
* **Canon Impact:** Vehicles run out of fuel; fuel costs fluctuate based on which faction is currently choking the Shinkai refineries. Damage to your vehicle does not disappear upon mission completion; every dent, blown tire, and cracked windshield must be paid for out of your gig earnings at Kotobuki garages.

### 6. The Wheelman Stays Behind the Wheel
* **The Rule:** The gameplay loop never turns into a third-person on-foot cover shooter. The vehicle is the player's body, weapon, shield, and livelihood.
* **Canon Impact:** Interactions with the world happen through the windows, the bumper, the horn, the trunk, and the radio. Violence is vehicular: pit maneuvers, ramming, roadblocks, and desperate high-speed escapes. When the driver steps out, it is during brief, stylized, cinematic handoff vignettes before the door slams shut and the ignition turns again.
