### (a) Media Inspirations

*   **Battles Without Honor and Humanity (1973, Kinji Fukasaku)**: Steal the feral, unglamorous desperation of post-war street soldiers fighting over petty port and scrap rackets, trading stoic cinema cool for raw factional infighting.
*   **Patlabor: The Movie (1989)**: Steal the weathered visual treatment of reclaimed industrial land—sprawling gantry cranes, rusted steel caissons, and construction barges set against hazy, sun-bleached watercolor skies.
*   **Megazone 23: Part I (1985)**: Steal the tactile cassette-futurist machinery—chunky dashboard hardware, raw carburetor induction roar, and late-Showa asphalt romance before the bubble burst.
*   **You're Under Arrest (1994 OVA)**: Steal the hyper-accurate mechanical vehicle draftsmanship and physics, where mundane compact municipal cars execute weight-shifting, tire-screeching open-road pursuits.
*   **Like a Dragon / Yakuza 0 (Game)**: Steal the tonal contrast rule: criminal syndicates and world systems treat absurd logistics (e.g., emergency transport of prize fighting fish or black-market mechanical parts) with total, deadpan gravity.
*   **Shenmue (Game)**: Steal the working-class rhythm of an authentic Showa-era port town—clanking chains, morning fog roll-ins, forklift-speed industrial navigation, and vending machines glowing in grease-stained alleys.
*   **Night on Earth (1991, Jim Jarmusch)**: Steal the cab interior framing as a high-stakes confessional booth where dangerous, eccentric faction figures talk in the rearview mirror while the driver silently navigates.
*   **Crazy Taxi (Game)**: Steal the sheer mechanical kineticism of curb-hopping pickups, passenger drop-off trajectory arcs, and reading road topology through street-level props.

### (b) Art Direction: Kurogane Bay Visual Bible

#### How This is NOT *Blade Runner* / NOT *Cyberpunk 2077*
*   **No holographic neon soup or rain-slicked purple/magenta alleys.** Kurogane Bay is daylight-forward, soot-stained, and sun-bleached.
*   **No microchips or cybernetics.** The technology is heavy, stamped-sheet steel, vacuum tubes, rubber hoses, cast iron, and bakelite.
*   **No rain-slicked wetness pass.** Shadows are sharp, dry, cel-inked cuts; highlights are hard painted gouache dabs, not ray-traced PBR reflections.

#### Overall Palette Rules
*   **Base Materials:** Weathered Showa industrial palette. Raw concrete grey (`#8A8D8F`), rust-primer oxide red (`#8B3A2B`), oxidized copper teal (`#4D7C78`), and grease-stained asphalt (`#2F3131`).
*   **Accent Tones:** High-saturation, flat anime cel inks. Cadmium highway yellow (`#E5A93C`), municipal sky blue (`#38779E`), and gas-discharge neon vermilion (`#D9381E`).
*   **Lighting Cut:** Exactly 2-step cel shading (Light and Shadow). Zero soft gradient ramps on geometric faces. Shadows take an ambient hue-shift toward cool indigo or sea-spray slate.

#### Per-District Signatures
*   **Daikoku Piers:**
    *   *Palette:* Rust-orange primer, peeling industrial sea-green, salt-white crust, diesel-soot black.
    *   *Materials & Shapes:* Corrugated sheet metal, shipping container stacks, lattice truss gantry cranes against wide-open pale skies.
    *   *Props:* Broken anchor chains, wooden dunnage piles, steam vents venting white cel-cut plumes.
*   **Kamome Wholesale Ward:**
    *   *Palette:* Wet tile-white, cold silver-blue, blood-apron vermilion, faded blue tarp canvas.
    *   *Materials & Shapes:* Low-slung wood-and-concrete market sheds, narrow multi-level catwalks, open floor drainage gullies.
    *   *Props:* Stacked styrofoam crates, crushed-ice bins, dangling raw incandescent work lamps, low-clearance motorized market carts.
*   **Chidori Neon Strip:**
    *   *Palette:* Deep night-navy base, cherry-red vacuum neon, cadmium amber, gold-lamé billboard paint.
    *   *Materials & Shapes:* Dense, narrow vertical corridors; low-poly building façades smothered in layered mechanical blade signs.
    *   *Props:* Overhanging air-conditioning chillers with spinning fans, thick hanging rubber utility cables, velvet club door drapes.
*   **Tenjin Mid-Corridor:**
    *   *Palette:* Monolithic granite slate, corporate bronze glass, dull aluminum, authoritative police-cruiser white.
    *   *Materials & Shapes:* Cold Showa brutalism—recessed window slits, massive square pillars, raised highway express viaducts that block the sun.
    *   *Props:* Monumental stone syndicate plaques, polished brass handrails, pneumatic document junction tubes running up exterior pillars.
*   **Kotobuki Slums & Repair Row:**
    *   *Palette:* Oxidized corrugated tin blue, oily motor brown, patchwork salvage yellow, raw asphalt.
    *   *Materials & Shapes:* Ramshackle, lean-to shanties bolted directly onto the round concrete pillars of the elevated expressway overhead.
    *   *Props:* Car engines hanging from chain hoists, stacked radial tires, smoking barrel fires, pneumatic delivery tubes patched with silver tape.
*   **Haibara Heights:**
    *   *Palette:* Terracotta roof-tile red, retaining-wall plaster white, rich maritime pine green, golden hour sunset amber.
    *   *Materials & Shapes:* Stepped retaining walls, cliffside switchbacks, gated Showa-modernist compound walls with heavy iron security gates.
    *   *Props:* Manicured pine branches leaning over stone walls, surveillance floodlights on brass brackets, parked luxury black sedans.
*   **Shinkai Basin:**
    *   *Palette:* Toxic sulfur yellow, bitumen black, flare-stack flame orange, dead brackish-water grey.
    *   *Materials & Shapes:* Enormous spherical gas storage tanks, tangled pipeline bridges crossing over roads, vertical distillation columns.
    *   *Props:* Continuously burning flare stack pilots (flat 2D flame billboards), warning signs with bold industrial glyphs, chemical valve stations.

#### Retro-Futurist Readability at Low-Poly
*   **Silhouettes over Surface Detail:** Don't model rivets. Use thick, stamped-steel geometric silhouettes—oversized cylindrical capsule pods, hexagonal pneumatic manifolds, octagonal junction boxes.
*   **Stencils as High-Contrast Visual Breaks:** Flat-color building faces receive bold, 2-color hand-stenciled Katakana utility markings (e.g., `高圧` - High Voltage, `危険` - Danger, `港湾局` - Port Authority) to break up empty low-poly surfaces without texturing noise.
*   **Tube Runways as World Connectors:** Exposed bundles of three to five thick, primary-color pneumatic tubes route along buildings and bridges, acting as visual breadcrumbs for road direction and district borders.

#### 1. Starter Beater Cab ("The Hinode Carrier")
*   **Silhouette:** Boxy 1970s notchback compact sedan with an aggressively high greenhouse, upright windshield, and stamped-steel utility bumper.
*   **Details:**
    *   Mechanical rooftop "VACANT" flag that physically snaps up and down via a roof-penetrating linkage.
    *   Dual chrome fender-mounted mirrors shaped like teardrop bullet pods.
    *   Rear passenger door painted in mismatched red oxide primer, fitted with an external pneumatic auto-open cylinder.

#### 2. Cargo Van ("Tatsumi Hauler")
*   **Silhouette:** Cab-over, forward-control flat-nose delivery van with an oversized cargo breadbox and dual rear wheel axles.
*   **Details:**
    *   Side-mounted exterior vertical rack carrying four swappable, pressurized brass pneumatic courier cylinders.
    *   Roof-mounted external air filter housing with an exposed, spinning paper-element cyclone intake.
    *   Horizontal split rear tailgate with heavy cast-iron locking bars and rubber bump stops.

#### 3. Luxury Sedan ("Ohtori Sovereign")
*   **Silhouette:** Stretched, low-slung brutalist executive cruiser with an endless hood, ultra-thick C-pillars, and zero rear quarter glass.
*   **Details:**
    *   Chrome-rimmed horizontal vacuum-tube taillight array running the full vehicle width.
    *   Rear side windows fitted with modeled low-poly pleated white privacy lace curtains.
    *   Front fender-mounted chrome pennant flag mounts for faction heraldry and syndicate crests.

#### 4. Tuner/Muscle Wedge ("Raiden Fastback")
*   **Silhouette:** Razor-edged fastback wedge profile featuring a pronounced front shark-nose, abrupt ducktail spoiler, and wide-flared box fenders.
*   **Details:**
    *   Twin dual-throat downdraft carburetors poking directly through an unpainted fiberglass hood cutout.
    *   Front-mounted external aluminum oil cooler with thick braided lines passing into the grill.
    *   Asymmetrical 4-spoke turbine wheels with ultra-deep dish rear rims and chunky white-lettered tires.

#### 5. Heavy Flatbed Rig ("Goliath 800")
*   **Silhouette:** Towering cab-over twin-steer tractor (four front steering wheels, four rear drive wheels) with an exposed structural ladder chassis flatbed.
*   **Details:**
    *   Dual vertical exhaust stacks behind the cab that pop thick, flat-shaded black smoke discs under engine load.
    *   Mid-mounted hydraulic knuckle-boom crane tucked behind the sleeper cab with oversized control levers.
    *   Stout front bumper featuring an integrated winch drum wrapped in steel cable, bracketed by massive amber fog lamps.

#### 6. Exotic Wedge ("Mirage Zero")
*   **Silhouette:** Ultra-low doorstop wedge inspired by experimental Japanese show cars; windshield and side glass form a single continuous canopy plane.
*   **Details:**
    *   Full-width motorized pop-up headlight brow that lifts straight up via mechanical jackscrews.
    *   Louvered matte-black rear engine deck sheltering a longitudinal mid-mounted engine with exposed orange silicone plug wires.
    *   Modular cast-magnesium wheels with turbine fan cooling disc covers bolted over the hubs.

### (d) UI/UX: The In-Dash CRT & In-Universe Readouts

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------+
|  [PUNCH-CARD ROUTE COMPUTER: KAMOME WARD -> CHIDORI STRIP] |
+-----------------------------------------------------------+
| +-------------------------+ [TAPE LOG] [PULL FARE FLAG]   |
| |  /\                     |                               |
| | /  \  [ROUTE ACTIVE]    | [SPEED] 068 KM/H              |
| | |  |                    | [METER] Y 2,400               |
| | \__/  O-==--X           | [VAC]   -18 KPA               |
| |                         |                               |
| +-------------------------+ (PUNCH CARD: [###  ##  ####]) |
+-----------------------------------------------------------+
```

#### 1. In-Dash CRT Route Computer
*   **Visual Form:** Molded into the center dash—a curved, thick glass 6-inch CRT screen held by an olive-drab or industrial beige bakelite bezel with visible flat-head mounting screws.
*   **Display Idiom:** P1 High-Persistence Green monochrome phosphor (`#33FF33` on `#102210`). The screen exhibits subtle scanline bands and a constant, low-frequency raster hum.
*   **Screen Behavior:** Route segments render as bright vector lines with a circular vector cursor representing the vehicle. The destination blinks with a crosshair (`[+]`). Vector lines trace onto the screen with a visible phosphor sweep beam rather than drawing instantly.
*   **Mechanical Interaction:** Below the CRT is a physical brass punch-card slot. Accepting a gig triggers a short 3D animation where the player feeds a rectangular card with punched hole patterns into the slot, accompanied by a heavy mechanical *CHUNK-CHUNK* sound.

#### 2. Gig-Planning Map (Pause/Preparation Screen)
*   **Visual Form:** Framed as an analog vector-storage oscilloscope display. The background is near-black slate with a stamped 10mm grid line overlay.
*   **Roads & Landmarks:** Districts are outlined in distinct phosphor line colors: Amber (`#FFB000`) for industrial zones, Marine Cyan (`#00FFFF`) for ports, Green (`#33FF33`) for corridors. Roads are crisp 2-pixel vector traces.
*   **Readability Safeguards (Mobile Screen):**
    *   No micro-text. Road names are replaced by bold district route numbers (e.g., `ROUTE 4`, `BAY LOOP`).
    *   Gig icons are oversized mechanical symbols: a heavy crate icon for cargo, a bowler hat/trenchcoat silhouette for syndicate fares, a wrench for chop-shop deliveries.
    *   Selected routes trace with high-contrast blinking vector arrows directly over the route lines.

#### 3. Driving HUD
*   **Taximeter / Fare Ticker:** Positioned at the top or top-right. Rendered as a mechanical rolling drum counter (split-flap / odometer style) where individual digits physically roll down with an audible mechanical *clack* as the fare climbs.
*   **Speedometer:** A large, circular dash gauge mirrored on the HUD edge: a thick white mechanical needle sweeping over high-contrast white hashmarks with a bold, legible numeric readout anchored in the center.
*   **Turn Indicators:** Large mechanical chevron arrows (`<<<` / `>>>`) flanking the dashboard edges that flash bright cadmium yellow with a distinct thermal-relay clicking sound.

### (e) Anime Look Fit: Frame-by-Frame Study Targets

#### 1. *Patlabor: The Movie* (1989) — Hiromasa Ogura (Art Director)
*   **Exact Technique to Steal: "Ogura Industrial Wash" for Environmental Backgrounds.**
    *   *Implementation:* Industrial structures (cranes, concrete retaining walls, container docks) are rendered using flat gouache base paints overlaid with dirty, sponge-textured rust washes and oil streaks along drainage lines. 
    *   *Application in Engine:* Model geometry uses clean, flat unlit colors, while environmental edges and drainage troughs use vertex color maps or decal cards carrying hard-edged, hand-drawn grime masks. Background mountains and harbor vistas drop contrast sharply into desaturated warm-grey atmospheric haze, preserving the ink lines of foreground buildings.

#### 2. *Akira* (1988) — Toshiharu Mizutani (Art Director)
*   **Exact Technique to Steal: Multi-Tiered Hard Cel Shadow Separation on Vehicles.**
    *   *Implementation:* Vehicles do not use soft shading across curved sheet metal. Instead, they use two distinct, hand-inked shadow levels: a core shadow (15% value drop, tone shifted toward blue-violet) and an ambient occlusion drop shadow (40% value drop, razor-sharp edge under chassis rails, wheel wells, and bumper seams).
    *   *Application in Engine:* A custom Godot 4 visual shader that maps surface normals to a stepped 2-band light map, coupled with an inverted-hull backface outline pass that scales thickness based on camera distance to maintain a uniform 1.5-pixel anime ink line.

#### 3. *Venus Wars* (1989) — Yoshikazu Yasuhiko (Director / Mecha Designer)
*   **Exact Technique to Steal: Mechanical Weight Compression & Dynamic Suspension Squat.**
    *   *Implementation:* Vehicle animation emphasizes the mechanical mass of heavy hardware. Tires flatten noticeably against the asphalt during high-G turns; during gear shifts and hard braking, chassis pitch forward aggressively on heavy coil springs.
    *   *Application in Engine:* Drive Godot’s vehicle suspension parameters to produce exaggerated chassis roll, dive, and squat on acceleration. Accentuate this with 2D animated tire-smoke discs (flat, circular hand-drawn anime puffs that rapidly scale and dissipate) rather than modern particle-system smoke clouds.
