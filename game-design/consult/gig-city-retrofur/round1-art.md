### QUESTION 2: Frame-by-Frame Visual DNA & Study List

#### 1. *You’re Under Arrest* (OVA 1994, Ep. 1 & 2) — Dir. Kazuhiro Furuhashi
* **The Exact Thing to Steal:** The hyper-fetishized mechanical interior drafting of compact Japanese utilitarian vehicles.
* **Asset Inspiration:** Look at the Honda Today police interceptor: the mechanical tactility of the climate control sliders, the manual window winders, the wiper blade linkages, and the realistic suspension travel when braking hard. Steal the way the engine bay is drawn with individual hose clamps, battery terminals, and greasy radiator caps.

#### 2. *Syd Mead’s Chronomaestro / 1980s Japanese Corporate Client Work*
* **The Exact Thing to Steal:** Mead’s corporate product designs for Bandai and JVC from 1983–1985.
* **Asset Inspiration:** The visual grammar of "Heavy Consumer Industrial." Mead uses large chamfers, warm-white/putty-beige ABS plastics, and recessed control clusters protected by raised rubber bumpers. Notice how he terminates vehicles with razor-flat vertical surfaces (kammbacks) rather than modern aerodynamic tapers.

```
       MEAD INDUSTRIAL CHAMFER                MODERN AERODYNAMIC (AVOID)
       +-----------------------+              . ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ .
      /                         \           /                 \
     /    [RECESS CONTROLS]      \         |    (Smooth Blob)  |
    +-----------------------------+         \                 /
    |                             |          ` ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ '
    +---\                     /---+          No distinct mechanical planes.
         \___________________/               Feels modern/hydroformed.
```

#### 3. Kenzo Tange & Kisho Kurokawa: Metabolism Movement & Expo ’70 (Osaka)
* **The Exact Thing to Steal:** Kurokawa’s Nakagin Capsule Tower joints and Tange’s Expo '70 Festival Plaza space frame.
* **Asset Inspiration:** 
  * Concrete is cast-in-place with visible form-tie holes and board-marked grain.
  * Modular capsule architecture where every room looks like a bolted-on shipping container with a rounded CRT-shaped window.
  * Giant tetrahedral space-frame roofs painted in white primer, now dripping with rust streaks, bridging the gap between skyscrapers over multi-lane elevated highways.

#### 4. Tsukuba Expo ’85 Official Pavilions & Promotional Catalogs
* **The Exact Thing to Steal:** The dawn of Japanese government-corporate tech optimism before the bubble burst.
* **Asset Inspiration:**
  * Giant, clunky track-mounted monorails with visible exposed bogies and leaf springs.
  * The aesthetic of early giant screens: the Sony Jumbotron at Expo '85 wasn't an OLED slate; it was an array of thousands of physical, individual Trinitron vacuum cells housed in a monolithic, corrugated steel tower.
  * Blocky, geometric, single-color line-art mascots screen-printed onto enameled metal signs.

#### 5. Shusei Nagaoka (Commercial Illustration & Album Art, 1978–1983)
* **The Exact Thing to Steal:** The lighting and rendering of specular chrome and painted sheet metal under direct, non-diffuse sunlight.
* **Asset Inspiration:**
  * Nagaoka renders metal not with blurry PBR roughness, but with razor-sharp horizon reflections: the top half of the chrome cylinder reflects a bleached, smoggy sky; the bottom half reflects warm ochre/terracotta ground.
  * Use his signature warm airbrushed halos around high-specular points—simulate this in-engine with a tight, non-bloom star-filter post-process.

#### 6. *Megazone 23* (Part 1, 1985) — Mechanical Design: Shinji Aramaki
* **The Exact Thing to Steal:** The Garland’s dashboard and transit interfaces.
* **Asset Inspiration:**
  * Physical segment LEDs (red and green only; zero blue/white).
  * Ribbed rubber gaiters over every shifting linkage, steering column, and hydraulic ram.
  * Decals using 1980s technical industrial English: "CAUTION: VENT VALVE BEFORE ACCESS", "EMERGENCY MANUAL RELEASE UNDER SEAT."

#### 7. Vintage Japanese Commercial Catalogs (Toyota HiAce / Crown, 1977–1983)
* **The Exact Thing to Steal:** The interior material palette and industrial two-tone vehicle paint separations.
* **Asset Inspiration:**
  * Exterior paint splits along the body-side rubber rub-strip: Olive Gold Metallic over Dark Green, or Desert Sand over Rust Brown.
  * Interiors dominated by monochromatic molded plastics in sickly, warm shades: maroon, slate blue, or cigarette-tar beige.

### QUESTION 3: The 3D Artist's Production Bible (Do / Don't)

#### Visual Taxonomy

| Feature | SHOWA RETRO-FUTURISM (Kurogane Bay) | GENERIC CYBERPUNK (Instant Fail) | GENERIC OLD-JAPAN (Instant Fail) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Dominant Lighting** | Hard, high-noon 2:00 PM sun; hazy ozone; smog diffusion. | Perpetual midnight; rain puddles; wet ground planes. | Overcast diffuse daylight or nostalgic golden hour. |

#### 1. Material Rules (Cel-Shading & Texturing Pipeline)

*   **DO: Limit Material Families to the Pre-Microchip Industrial Era.**
    *   **Metals:** Stamped cold-rolled steel (with visible spot-weld divots), unpolished cast iron, galvanized zinc dipping (with crystalline spangle pattern), and chrome that reflects simple environmental horizons.
    *   **Plastics:** Phenolic resin/Bakelite (dark brown/amber, brittle, glossy), early injection-molded ABS (thick-walled, slightly uneven, yellowed by UV exposure), and clear amber/smoked-green acrylics for gauge covers.
    *   **Seals & Hoses:** Everything hydraulic or mechanical needs visible thick vulcanized black rubber hoses clamped with worm-gear steel bands.
*   **DON'T: Use Carbon Fiber, Kevlar, or Seamless Injection Molding.**
    *   If a surface is curved, it was either pressed on a multi-ton mechanical punch press (meaning it has distinct draw-lines and stiffener ribs) or it is an assembled series of flat planes.
*   **DO: Model the Fasteners and Access Panels.**
    *   Assets should feature visible, oversized cross-head (JIS) screws, hex-head bolts, cotter pins, and safety-wire assemblies. Machines are maintained by men with wrenches, not swapped out as sealed modular cartridges.

#### 2. Lighting Rules (Cel-Shading Shader Directives)

*   **DO: High-Contrast, Bleached Daylight.**
    *   Set the directional light between **60° and 75° elevation**. The sun is harsh, unforgiving, and white-yellow.
    *   Use a **2-band or 3-band stepped cel-shader**. The shadow band must not be cool blue/purple (that is the standard anime trope); the shadow band must be a **warm, soot-tinted umber or industrial greenish-grey**, simulating light bouncing off dirty concrete and leaded gasoline exhaust.
*   **DON'T: Rain-Slicked Midnight.**
    *   Ground surfaces must be dry, cracked, and covered in an accumulated layer of diesel particulate dust. Puddles are rare, oily, and exhibit rainbow thin-film interference, rather than clean, mirror-like reflections.
*   **DO: Emissive Restraint.**
    *   Emissives are only allowed if they represent an active filament bulb, a P1 (green) or P4 (white) phosphor CRT, or a high-voltage vacuum tube. The light emission must not cast wide, soft volumetric glows. It should be harsh, local, and clipped.

#### 3. Common Failure Modes to Reject at Review

*   **Failure Mode 1: The "Neo-Tokyo" Trap (Blade Runner Drift)**
    *   *The Symptom:* Artist submits an alleyway covered in glowing neon kanji signs, dark rainy shadows, and pink/cyan edge highlights.
    *   *The Fix:* Strip all neon. Replace it with **enamel-painted metal signs illuminated from above by a hooded gooseneck incandescent lamp**. Change the time of day to 13:45. Make the sky an overexposed light slate-gray with an amber rim near the horizon.
*   **Failure Mode 2: The "Ghost in the Shell 95" Drift (Digital Military)**
    *   *The Symptom:* Clean, tactical, low-profile chassis with angular drone-like facets, digital camouflage, or modern sensor blisters.
    *   *The Fix:* Add a carburetor air-intake scoop. Replace the sensor ball with an articulated glass-lensed searchlight controlled by a physical interior push-rod. Add external structural ribs to the panels.
*   **Failure Mode 3: The "Chowa/Nostalgia" Trap (Generic Old-Japan)**
    *   *The Symptom:* Asset looks like a traditional Showa-era nostalgic countryside film—wooden utility poles, rusted tin roofs, sliding shoji doors, peaceful ramen stands.
    *   *The Fix:* Scale it up industrially. Replace wooden poles with **brutal, over-engineered pre-stressed concrete utility pylons** carrying 30 individual step-down transformers. This is an alternate industrial super-power, not a pastoral memory: it should feel heavy, dense, and aggressively modernized under 1970s civil engineering plans.
*   **Failure Mode 4: Form-Factor Anachronism (Flat Screens & Soft Corners)**
    *   *The Symptom:* A computer terminal with an LCD profile (flat screen, thin bezel) or vehicle dashboards with rounded, organic ergonomics.
    *   *The Fix:* The display must have a **spherical curved glass CRT face** with heavy interior parallax shadow from the bezel. Controls must be discrete: rocker switches, thumbwheels, bakelite rotary dials, and clunky push-buttons that depress 15 millimeters into the housing.
