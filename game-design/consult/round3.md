### 1. RETRO-FUTURISM WORLD BIBLE (Direction D)

*   **Architecture (Foundations):** Modular, cellular concrete-and-steel megastructures with visible service cores and pod living units (*Ref: Kisho Kurokawa’s Nakagin Capsule Tower, Metabolism Movement*).
*   **Architecture (Infrastructure):** High-elevation geometric transit viaducts, elevated plazas, and monumental pavilion arches (*Ref: Tsukuba Expo ’85 site masterplan / Kenzo Tange*).
*   **Vehicles (Exterior):** Aerodynamic geometric wedge silhouettes, low drag coefficients, active canopy cockpits, flush glass, and perimeter rubber bumper strips (*Ref: 1985 Tokyo Motor Show prototypes: Nissan CUE-X, Mazda MX-03*).
*   **Vehicles (Interior):** Heavy tactical switches, glowing amber Vacuum Fluorescent Displays (VFD), CRT navigation blocks, and twin-grip aircraft-style yokes (*Ref: Leiji Matsumoto’s Space Battleship Yamato bridge consoles*).
*   **Space & Scale:** Massive interior curves of rotating habitats, overhead artificial sky arrays, and towering structural ribs (*Ref: Mobile Suit Gundam early O'Neill cylinder Colony Side 7 designs*).
*   **Signage & Typography:** Katakana-heavy technical nomenclature, international warning glyphs, high-contrast block lettering, zero graffiti (*Ref: Syd Mead’s Kronolog and Japanese concept sketches*).
*   **Sky & Atmosphere:** Industrial cobalt-blue gradient bleeding into deep space black; crisp, dustless horizon lit by artificial secondary sun-mirrors.
*   **Soundscape:** Polyphonic analog synth pads, Yamaha DX7 slap-bass accents, high-frequency electric turbine whine, and physical pneumatic air-release chuffs on gear changes.
*   **Color Rules:** Strict triad of Bone White, Signal Orange, and Cobalt Blue on Brushed Gunmetal. Zero magenta/cyan neon vomit.

**The Mechanical Shift:**
*   **Core Verb:** **HARVEST** (not *Deliver* or *Outrun*).
*   **Mechanic:** **Thermal Vectoring.** You are test-piloting unstable, high-output prototypes. Rather than racing point-to-point with fragile cargo, you draft along infrastructure conduits, surf inductive power-strips, and perform extreme high-G vector turns to keep a volatile core balanced between "Maximum Output" and "Critical Overheat." You don’t drop packages off; you skim track nodes to offload thermal energy before the chassis melts.

### 2. COMEDY-FORWARD: STEELMAN & VERDICT

**The Steelman:**
Make the core loop *Roguelike Hazard Choreography*. You drive a 40-second high-speed obstacle gauntlet where your vehicle gains procedurally paired, physically simulated absurdity: e.g., *Tuna on the Roof* (shifts center of mass violently on drift) + *Overpowered Left Turbine* + *Greased Brakes*. 

The joke isn't dialogue; it’s emergent slapstick physics failure. Humor stays fresh via modular, synergistic modifier stacking—players try to conquer impossible physical dysfunctions. It works on mobile because failures create instantly shareable, 5-second TikTok-ready ragdoll clips requiring zero linguistic context.

**Verdict: KILL IT.**
Visual gags decay after three exposures. Building enough unique physics modifiers to sustain 20 hours requires triple the content budget of a tight arcade racer. You will run out of jokes before the player runs out of battery.

### 4. THE ACCURACY SHIELD

Three strict production rules to eliminate history-nerd nitpicking instantly:

1.  **The "District 8X" Fictionalization Rule:** Never use real ward or route names (no "C1 Loop" or "Shibuya"). Name them with industrial codes: *Sector 9 Port Route*, *Capital Viaduct 4*. Nerds can’t cite incorrect off-ramps if the map doesn't exist on Google.
2.  **The Frankenstein-Spec Policy:** No real manufacturer badges or 1:1 body panels. Splice visual DNA: graft an AE86 rear hatch onto an S12 Silvia front clip and label it the *Yamato Twin-Cam 16*. History nerds can’t complain about the wrong taillights on a car that was never built.
3.  **The Explicit Hard-Fork Date Splash:** Establish an explicit alternate history card on initial boot: *"1988: In the wake of the Extended Bubble..."* Establishing a parallel economic reality upfront gives you complete immunity to pull tech, culture, and aesthetics from anywhere between 1978 and 1994 without consequence.
